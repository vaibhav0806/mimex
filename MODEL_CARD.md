# Mimex HC3 baseline

This records the original v1 experiment. Historical v2 results are in
[EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md); the active v3 research checkpoint is documented in
[EVALUATION_V3.md](EVALUATION_V3.md).

Local experiment completed on 2026-10-29. This is an independent research
baseline; it has not been shown to match Pangram or generalize to current LLMs.

## Model and training

- Base: `answerdotai/ModernBERT-base`, approximately 149.6 million parameters.
- Base revision: `8949b909ec900327062f0ebf497f51aef5e6f0c8`.
- Full-parameter fine-tuning: 2 epochs, batch size 2, AdamW, learning rate 2e-5,
  gradient clipping at 1.0, seed 42, float32, SDPA, 256-token input limit.
- Machine: Apple M4 Pro, 24 GB unified memory; PyTorch MPS.
- Training, calibration and checkpoint saving: **356.97 seconds** (excludes
  dataset/model download and separate test evaluation).
- Local artifact: `checkpoints/hc3/`; provenance and calibration in `mimex.json`.

## Dataset and separation

[HC3](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Hello-SimpleAI/HC3), by Guo et al.,
*How Close is ChatGPT to Human Experts? Comparison Corpus, Evaluation, and
Detection* ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07597)), CC-BY-SA-4.0.
Revision: `4d0ff18143b5a7e1b1e79beb540c04549d1e59d3`.

Selected 1,500 question pairs with one human-labelled and one ChatGPT-labelled
answer of 50–600 words each. Both classes receive the same whitespace,
punctuation-spacing and English-contraction normalization. Exact normalized
answer duplicates are excluded; questions are grouped before splitting.

| Split | Human | ChatGPT | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Training | 1,050 | 1,050 | 2,100 |
| Threshold calibration | 225 | 225 | 450 |
| Held-out test | 225 | 225 | 450 |

File hashes are checked before training/evaluation. Human and AI answers to a
question remain in the same partition. The split is question-disjoint, **not**
author-disjoint or corpus-disjoint. Near duplicates have not been audited.

## Fixed-threshold evaluation

Threshold **0.000069815490860492** was selected using calibration human answers,
with an empirical false-positive target of at most 1%. Calibration produced
2/225 human false positives and detected 224/225 AI answers. The classifier's
softmax outputs are highly polarized; its numerical score is not a calibrated
probability of AI authorship.

The threshold and model were fixed before evaluating the held-out test set.
No test-driven retraining or threshold adjustment was performed.

| Held-out metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Correct AI detections | 225 / 225 (100%) |
| Human answers incorrectly flagged | 4 / 225 (1.78%) |
| Human answers below threshold | 221 / 225 |
| AI answers missed | 0 / 225 |
| Overall accuracy on this balanced set | 99.11% |
| Human false-positive rate, 95% Wilson interval | 0.69%–4.48% |

The **1% calibration target did not hold on the test set**. The sample is too
small to establish a reliable sub-1% deployment false-positive rate. Zero AI
misses in this sample does not guarantee perfect future detection.

| Test source | Human examples | Human false positives | AI detections / AI examples |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Finance | 44 | 2 | 44 / 44 |
| Medicine | 11 | 0 | 11 / 11 |
| Open QA | 2 | 1 | 2 / 2 |
| Reddit ELI5 | 159 | 1 | 159 / 159 |
| Wiki CSAI | 9 | 0 | 9 / 9 |

Some subgroups are extremely small. Results are dominated by Reddit ELI5.
Full per-source uncertainty is in `runs/evaluation.json`.

An additional browser smoke check used a new paragraph generated by the coding
assistant during implementation. It fell below the AI threshold (a missed AI
example). This single out-of-domain example is not a benchmark, but demonstrates
why the strong HC3 result must not be presented as general detection accuracy.
The paragraph and result are visible in `runs/browser-check.png`.

## Software verification

Five unit tests passed, covering split leakage, normalization, confusion counts,
threshold ties and false-positive uncertainty. Ruff lint and formatting checks
passed. CLI prediction, HTTP prediction, short-input handling and invalid-input
handling were exercised against the saved model. Chrome successfully completed
an analysis through the interface. These are functional checks, separate from
model-quality evaluation.

## Intended use and limitations

Use for local experimentation and development of a reproducible detection
pipeline. Predictions run locally after the initial downloads. The UI and CLI
require at least 50 words and disclose truncation beyond 256 tokens.

- Older ChatGPT answers from one dataset are not a contemporary multi-model
  benchmark. Current generators, other languages and arbitrary prose are untested.
- Dataset labels are inherited from HC3, not independently verified here.
- Shared corpus conventions, residual style/formatting clues and length
  differences may make this task easier than real-world detection.
- No author-disjoint evaluation, near-duplicate audit, external benchmark,
  paraphrase robustness test or independent replication has been completed.
- A document's later tokens are not analyzed. Mixed and AI-assisted writing
  cannot be reliably classified by this experiment.
- Scores and flags do not prove authorship; below threshold does not prove human
  authorship. Do not use this prototype as the sole basis for punitive decisions.
- Code is MIT; base weights and training data have their own licenses. Neither
  datasets nor trained weights are bundled in Git or published by this setup.

Next research milestone: collect licensed, matched examples from contemporary
models and human authors, then evaluate on independently sourced, author-disjoint
holdouts. Hard-negative mining must use training-only data; the current test set
must remain outside model/threshold selection.
